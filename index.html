ScriptedOT

Recognizable dramatic scenes from film and television, used to introduce complex cyber-physical concepts — then grounded in documented industrial incidents, ISA/IEC 62443 and NIST SP 800-82, and reproducible engineering runbooks.

● 10 of 10 modules live Domains: Nuclear, Power Generation, Water, Building Automation, Oil & Gas, Aerospace, Maritime Defense, Traffic Control, Healthcare Facilities
How a module works
Every module follows the same four-part structure, moving from fiction to field-verified fact.
01 — The Script
Cinematic Anchor
A high-tension scene, mapped character-by-character onto real control-room and plant-floor roles.
02 — Theory
ICS Architecture
The scenario broken down by Purdue Model level, with the protocols and control logic actually in play.
03 — The Plant
Empirical Grounding
A documented real-world incident, a cinematic-vs-reality matrix, and a formal root-cause classification.
04 — Runbook
Countermeasures
Telemetry envelopes, a step-by-step mitigation/recovery protocol, and the applicable ISA/IEC/NIST clauses.
Modules
Ten scenes, ten domains, ten real incidents.
Module 01 · Nuclear Power Generation Live Chernobyl (2019) Positive void coefficient, SIS override, safety-envelope violation.
Module 02 · Nuclear Power Generation Live The China Syndrome (1979) Telemetry divergence between sensor feedback and true physical state; stuck PORV.
Module 03 · Building & Perimeter Automation Live Jurassic Park (1993) Insider threat, undocumented backdoor script, cold-start recovery failure.

Module 06 · Maritime / Defense Systems Live Battlestar Galactica (2004) IT/OT convergence risk vs. intentional air-gapping; supply-chain compromise of field controllers.
Module 07 · Traffic Management Live The Italian Job (2003) Master-station compromise, spoofed detector feeds, unauthenticated legacy field controllers.
Module 08 · Aerospace / Microgrid Power Live Apollo 13 (1995) Constrained power budgets, manual sequencing, black-start recovery under failure.
Module 09 · Oil & Gas / Offshore Drilling Live Deepwater Horizon (2016) Alarm normalization, sensor misinterpretation, SIS/ESD failure at the final protection layer.
Module 10 · Hospital Building Automation Live The Pitt (2025) Alarm fatigue, medical gas manifold monitoring, automatic transfer switch failover logic.
