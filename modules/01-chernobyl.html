Diegetic Failure Mode

A turbine-rundown safety test is resumed after an unplanned delay, driving the reactor through a low-power operating region the RBMK was never validated for. Xenon-135 poisoning suppresses reactivity; operators withdraw control rods far beyond the permitted margin to compensate, masking the true state of the core. When the emergency SCRAM (AZ-5) is finally triggered, the control rods' graphite displacer tips push coolant out of the lower channels before the neutron-absorbing boron sections enter — inserting positive reactivity instead of shutting the reactor down. Power spikes to roughly 100× rated output in seconds; two explosions destroy the reactor core.

Section 02

Theoretical ICS Architecture

Purdue Model Impact

Level 0 — Field Devices Control rods (200 manual + automatic regulator rods), graphite displacer tips, fuel channels, main circulating pumps (8 total, 8 running vs. recommended max), neutron flux ion chambers, pressure/steam-quality sensors in the separator drums.
Level 1 — Basic Control (PLC-equivalent) Local Automatic Regulator (LAR/AR) rod-position control loops; the AZ-5 emergency protection system — a hardwired, relay-logic latching trip that drives all control/emergency rods to full insertion. No microprocessor logic; this is electromechanical control predating digital fieldbus.
Level 2 — Supervisory / HMI Unit 4 control room instrument panel: neutron flux recorders (unreliable at the low power the test was run at), reactivity margin indication (SKALA computer, advisory only — not interlocked to force a trip), steam/water parameters on analog gauges.
Level 3 — Site Operations Plant-wide test scheduling and shift coordination between Unit 4 operations and the Kyiv grid dispatcher, who ordered the test delayed by ~9 hours — pushing it into a night shift with a less-experienced crew.

Protocols & Control Logic

This is a pre-digital OT environment: no Modbus, DNP3, or OPC UA — control was hardwired analog/relay logic with electromechanical rod drive mechanisms. The relevant control concepts are:

  • PID-equivalent control: the Local Automatic Regulator held neutron flux at a setpoint via continuous rod position adjustment.
  • Latching interlock (SIS-equivalent): AZ-5 was designed as the final independent protection layer — a one-way trip with no automatic reset, conceptually analogous to a modern IEC 61511 Safety Instrumented Function (SIF).
  • Missing interlock: there was no automatic trip on low Operational Reactivity Margin (ORM). Operators could — and did — withdraw rods below the documented safe minimum with no independent system stopping them. In a modern SIS design this would be a mandatory, operator-independent Safety Integrity Level (SIL) rated trip.
Section 03

The Plant — Empirical Grounding

Real-World Incident Precedent

Chernobyl (2019) is a dramatization of an actual event, not a fictionalized analog — the Unit 4 accident of 26 April 1986. The authoritative technical reconstruction is the IAEA's INSAG-7 (1992), which superseded the original 1986 INSAG-1 report after establishing that RBMK design flaws — not operator error alone — were a primary cause. Two design defects converge here: a positive void coefficient (as coolant boils to steam voids at low power, reactivity increases rather than decreases) and the positive scram effect of graphite-tipped control rods, which briefly increased reactivity on insertion instead of reducing it.

Failsafe Behavior AZ-5 is shown as the point of no return — the "safety" button that triggers the explosion. Documented in INSAG-7: AZ-5 was the designed final protection layer, but the rod tip geometry meant its own actuation inserted positive reactivity before absorption began — a safety system that briefly became the initiating event.

Root-Cause Analysis (RCA)

Classification: Process/Reactor Design + Human Factors

Primary root cause is process design: an SIS component (the AZ-5 trip) capable of causing the very excursion it was meant to arrest, combined with the absence of an automatic, operator-independent interlock on reactivity margin. This combines with human factors: a test executed under schedule and authority pressure, in violation of the documented minimum operating reactivity margin, by a shift crew not fully briefed on the test program. This was not a network or cyber failure — there was no supervisory network to compromise — which is itself the lesson: rigorous interlock design matters independently of any cyber threat model.

Mitigation / Recovery Protocol

  1. Independent SIS trip on reactivity margin: implement an automatic, operator-independent reactor trip when ORM falls below the documented safe minimum — decoupled from the Basic Process Control System (BPCS) per IEC 61511 independence-of-layers principle.
  2. Eliminate positive-scram-effect geometry: redesign control rod/absorber assemblies so trip actuation cannot insert positive reactivity under any coolant condition.
  3. Xenon transient hold procedure: mandate a minimum stabilization period following any significant power reduction before resuming low-power testing.
  4. Management of Change (MOC) gate: require independent, documented authorization for any deviation from an approved test procedure — no single on-shift authority may waive a safety-margin requirement.
  5. Post-event fail-safe activation and cold shutdown: in the actual event, this step failed; the corrective program that followed added ORM interlocks and reduced rod insertion time to ~12 seconds fleet-wide.

Standards Reference

IEC 61511-1, Cl. 11 (SIS design) — requires the Safety Instrumented Function to be independent of the Basic Process Control System; the AZ-5/BPCS relationship at Unit 4 did not meet this separation in practice.
IEC 61508 (Functional Safety of E/E/PE Systems) — the positive-scram-effect rod design is a textbook case of a safety function that fails to meet its own safety integrity requirement under specific process conditions.
ISA-84 (MOC provisions) — management-of-change discipline for any test or procedure deviating from the approved safety case, with documented, independent sign-off.
Scope note — this incident is a process-safety/SIS design failure, not a cyber incident. ISA/IEC 62443 (industrial cybersecurity) is not directly applicable; it is referenced elsewhere in ScriptedOT for network/cyber-vector modules.
